# Original pilot script seed

Original Luma demonstration text, prepared for a future approved speaker session. These lines are a small quality/evaluation seed, not a complete training corpus. Do not record until speaker consent, retention and rights terms are agreed. Names and situations below are fictional. Read naturally in your own voice; do not imitate a known person. Do not read the headings or line numbers aloud.

## Calm reading

1. The morning light reached the desk before the room grew warm.
2. I left a space beside the paragraph for a question I could answer later.
3. A clear explanation can begin with a small, ordinary example.
4. We paused at the bridge and listened to water moving over the stones.
5. Keep the original page nearby while you compare the two accounts.

## Questions and contrast

6. Which part of the plan would you change first?
7. Did the second reading make the difficult sentence easier to follow?
8. The answer was simple, but the reason took another moment to explain.
9. Read slowly enough to notice a change in meaning.
10. Please save the note before closing the window.

## Dates, titles and numbers — evaluate normalization separately

11. Dr. Lema met Mr. Hale on the eighteenth of October.
12. The balance increased from forty-two dollars to fifty-seven dollars.
13. Three point one four is an approximation, not the entire number.
14. Chapter twelve begins on page one hundred and six.
15. The session lasted twenty-five minutes, followed by a short break.

## Consonants, rhythm and longer passages

16. Bright glass jars stood beside a wooden box of fresh green leaves.
17. The quiet rhythm of the workshop changed when the doors opened.
18. A practical question is often more useful than a perfect heading.
19. After listening to the passage, write one sentence in your own words. Check whether it says what the author meant, then return to the page for anything you missed.
20. Tomorrow we will review the notes, correct the uncertain amounts, and decide which question deserves another reading.

Reserve several lines and new passages exclusively for evaluation. For numeric tests, pair the spoken sentences above with raw written forms in a separate normalization benchmark; do not mix spoken transcripts and raw numeric text silently in training metadata. Add language/accent-specific phonetic coverage only after the speaker and training specialist review the pilot.
